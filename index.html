02 · The dial

Bodies orbit by when they're due.

The nearer the ring, the sooner.

Today sits at twelve o'clock. Each day of lead time is one degree round; distance grows with time. Overdue falls inward, toward the sun. A bigger body costs more. The law is the same for every household and never changes.

Hover a body on the dial above to read it. Every position up there is the law's own output for a sample household, not a picture of one.

03 · Add

Add anything, by hand or by forwarding a document.

A type, a section, a date. Or drop the paper and let Orbit read the dates, amounts and reference numbers out of it.

Anything Orbit inferred is drawn as a proposal, with a dashed ring and an explicit accept. Nothing it guessed becomes true until you say so.

drop a document — we'll read what we can
04 · The belt

Every body carries its documents in a belt around it.

The belt is what you have attached to it.

Open a body and everything Orbit holds about it is there: when it's due, its orbital period, what it costs, the reminders, and the papers riding with it. The page itself, read without leaving the sky.

05 · Below the dial

The manifest lists what's ahead, nearest first.

Same law as the dial, read top to bottom instead of round the ring.

Needs attention, then later this year, month by month. Complete, renew, reschedule or snooze a row in place, and Orbit works out the next date for anything that comes round again. A repeat is never finished; it comes round. A one-off simply ends.

In your pocket the whole sky fits at phone width, and installs to the home screen as a web app.

06 · Paper by post

Forward a bill to your relay address and Orbit reads a copy.

Your mail is never redirected — it keeps arriving exactly where it always has.

Every account gets a private relay address of its own. Send a renewal, a receipt or a certificate there and it lands in your review queue with the dates already read.

07 · Inbox

Mail lands here first.

Filed, waiting for review, or still being read. Nothing joins your orbit without your say-so.

What Orbit could file is filed against the body it belongs to. What it isn't sure about waits for you, with its reading beside it. What it couldn't read at all says so, plainly, and keeps the original.

08 · Other households

That's your sun, at centre — your household, always here.

The rest of the sky holds systems you don't belong to — tap one to fly there.

A cottage, a relative's flat, the boat. Each household is its own system in one fixed sky, at a bearing that never moves, with its own members and its own orbit. Owners add people by display name; nobody's email is exposed.

09 · Your sky

Five skies. One identity.

Star chart, after dark, clouds, dawn and retrograde. The same orbit under a different light.

Dark is the identity; the daylight skies are derived from the same tokens, not inverted. Every one clears the contrast law as words on its own ground. Try one on this page — the site wears the packs the app does.

10 · Yours

One app. Standard supporting services. Your server.

Orbit runs on a machine you control. There is no hosted tenancy and no phone-home for your data.

The footprint is deliberately small: the whole application in one container, the official PostgreSQL image pinned by digest, a document volume that only ever sees ciphertext, and the official scanner fed a quarantined stream on a private network. Backups, restores and a recovery bundle for the keys are part of the product.

11 · Get into Orbit

One line, then a guided install.

On a Linux host with Docker Compose, one command downloads a signed manifest, verifies the launcher and the installer against it, and only then hands over.

The launcher walks you through the rest in its own console, showing every step of the installer as it runs, and gives you the address when the deployment is healthy.
